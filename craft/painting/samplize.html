A Quick Trick for Choosing the Right Color

A simple way to see how a color really lives in your home

Choosing paint can feel abstract until you see the color living on your walls. A peel‑and‑stick sample makes that part simple. Instead of buying sample pots or painting test patches you later have to cover, you can place a large sheet of real paint on the wall and watch how it shifts throughout the day.

Pillar: Craft · Painting

Why This Trick Works

Samplize makes 9" × 15" removable samples using actual paint from Sherwin‑Williams, Benjamin Moore, and Farrow & Ball. They’re thin, flexible, and easy to move from room to room—north light, south light, next to trim, beside tile, or across from a window. That movement is the trick. It shows you the undertones you’d never catch on a paper chip and helps you see how a color behaves in the spaces you actually live in.

These samples have been invaluable in our last several homes. They helped narrow down the quieter whites for bedrooms and bathrooms, and they were essential in choosing the main house color in Pennsylvania—Sherwin‑Williams Shoji White—a shade that only made sense once we saw it in morning sun, afternoon shade, and under warm evening light. They’re especially helpful for comparing whites and off‑whites, where subtle shifts in undertone only reveal themselves at scale.

At $6.95 each, they’re a small investment that prevents expensive mistakes and makes the whole process easier. You can explore their full collection at samplize.com.
