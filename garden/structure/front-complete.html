Bringing Our Initial Vision to Life: Completing the Front Beds

The Living Structure · Late Summer 2026

Earlier this year, we took our first tentative steps toward reclaiming the front of the house from decades of being an afterthought. What began as a single focused bed near the front porch has now grown into a complete transformation. This September, we finished the entire right-hand span of the front landscape—stretching all the way from the front door over to the driveway.

We used an edging spade to remove grass and weeds and to define the soft curves of the new bed. However, opening up the ground in front of the dining room presented its usual challenges: the surface soil was rock-hard (not surprising given the trampling from recent renovation) and a standard shovel did not make a dent. Thank goodness for our EGO battery-operated auger, which made digging holes for the plants an absolute breeze. We were pleasantly surprised to find good soil underneath. The auger gave us holes large enough to provide plant holes and plenty of surrounding good soil.

Moving further down toward the garage windows, the area had become a tangle of encroaching grass, an old tree stump (since removed), and an isolated clump of sedum which we carefully dug up and relocated to the rear yard. Amidst the overgrowth, however, we found patches of mallow and hollyhock that had been coming up on their own for years. Instead of clearing them out, we chose to keep and integrate them directly into the new final design as living testaments to the land's resilience. They are now thriving, as if they appreciate the water and nutrients they haven't had in a long time. In addition, the hollyhock's taller and more organic growth provides a fitting end-stop for the bed at the driveway's edge.

Shaping and Planting the Space

When cutting the new bed lines in front of the garage, we tried to adopt a natural, sloping S-curve inspired by the proportions of the golden ratio, giving the edge a flowing, organic grace rather than a rigid geometric line. As we stripped the grass, it went straight into our wagon—repurposed immediately to help start our new hugelkultur beds elsewhere on the property.

For the plant selection, we leaned entirely into hardy perennials designed to handle our northwest Pennsylvania climate while putting on a heavy pollinator display. The plants all came from our local Lowe's store in Hermitage, PA. The palette features dependable Coneflower, striking red lobelia, Emerald Crest bluebeard, Russian sage, and Rose Marvel sage. Tucked neatly into the architectural corner where the dining room wall meets the garage wall, we also added a handsome hydrangea to anchor the transition.

We followed the fibonacci sequence when designing plant layout (1, 2, 3, 5, etc.). 1 hydrangea in the corner to provide visual weight and add texture. Intermittently placing 2 of the same plant next to each other as part of a grouping of 3s. This strategy also led to us having 5 salvia plants in the bed underneath the dining room windows where 3 groupings of 3 plants came together in the final design. With the final plants in the ground, the entire front of the house finally matches the vision we’ve been building toward.

Fast forward to the week before Thanksgiving... We've already had severe frost but these plants are still hanging in there. We believe they benefit from the radiant heat captured by the house during the day, but the coneflowers are starting to brown as they send nutrients down into their roots. We hope these roots have had plenty of time to settle in and hunker down for the winter months ahead, setting the stage for a marvelous, buzzing pollinator garden come next spring. More to come!
