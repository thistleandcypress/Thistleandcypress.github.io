How We Cook

Our kitchen holds two instincts that meet at the same table. One of us cooks intuitively, guided by memory and instinct, turning whatever's on hand into something nourishing without ever consulting a recipe. The other moves more slowly, drawn to the meditative work of baking and fresh pasta — the grounding rhythm of flour, dough, and repetition.

We use Italian 00 and semolina flours — wheat grown and milled with care, free from the heavy processing common elsewhere. It's a small choice that reflects how we want to nourish ourselves: with intention, from trusted sources, honoring both craft and origin.

We pay attention to where our food comes from. We follow the Dirty Dozen and Clean Fifteen carefully, choosing organic when it matters most and celebrating ingredients that shine in their natural state. When we travel, we seek out farmers markets — not just for what we find, but for the glimpse they offer into a place's rhythm, soil, and season.

At home, the weekly farmers market has become ritual. We wander the stalls, taste what's new, and let the colors and scents shape the meals ahead. It's a reminder that cooking isn't just about technique — it's about connection, seasonality, and the quiet joy of choosing what feels right for the moment we're in.

This is the heart of our kitchen: intuitive cooking, mindful sourcing, and the simple pleasure of making food that feels honest and alive.
