Agnolotti with Two Fillings & Brown Butter

Kitchen · Cooking · Serves 5

A New Year’s ritual: prepare and freeze handmade agnolotti on New Year’s Eve, then cook them gently in brown butter and herbs on New Year’s Day.

Prep: New Year’s Eve · Cook: New Year’s Day

Overview

Handmade agnolotti with two traditional fillings — potato and cheese, and sauerkraut with mushroom — finished in nutty brown butter with sage and thyme.

Every kitchen behaves differently—use your own judgment with ingredients, allergens, and doneness cues.

Dough

  • 400 g Italian 00 flour
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1–2 tsp olive oil (optional)
  • Pinch of salt

Potato & Cheese Filling

  • 3–4 lbs potatoes (russet or Yukon Gold)
  • 1 yellow onion, finely diced
  • 2–4 tbsp butter
  • 6–8 oz sharp cheddar or farmer’s cheese
  • Salt and black pepper

Sauerkraut & Mushroom Filling

  • 16 oz sauerkraut, drained
  • 8 oz mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 1 yellow onion, finely diced
  • 2–3 tbsp butter or olive oil
  • 1–2 tsp caraway seeds (optional)
  • Splash white wine or broth (optional)
  • Salt and black pepper

Brown Butter Herb Sauce

  • 8–12 tbsp butter
  • 8–12 fresh sage leaves
  • 3-4 sprigs of thyme leaves (strip the leaves off the stems first)
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Freshly grated Parmigiano Reggiano

Method

1. Make the Dough

  1. Form flour into a mound and create a well.
  2. Add eggs, salt, and olive oil into the center.
  3. Beat eggs with a fork, gradually incorporating flour.
  4. Remember that your hands bedt tool; don't be afraid to get messy
  5. Knead the dough for 10 minutes-until smooth and elastic.
  6. Wrap tightly and rest 30–60 minutes (counter or fridge is fine).

2. Prepare Fillings

Potato & Cheese: Boil potatoes until fork tender. Sauté onion in butter until translucent. Mash potatoes (we prefer a ricer) and combine with onion and cheese. The heat from the potatoes will melt the cheese. Season generously with salt and pepper. Cool completely.

Sauerkraut & Mushroom: Sauté onion and mushrooms until browned. Add sauerkraut and optional seasonings. Cook 5–10 minutes. Cool completely.

3. Roll & Shape

  1. Divide dough into 4 pieces.
  2. Dust your baking mat or work surface with semolina so the pasta does not stick
  3. Roll thin using pasta machine (start with setting 1 and gradually increase until setting 6–7).
  4. Cut into 2–2.5 inch squares; I prefer to use a ravioli stamp.
  5. Spoon 1 tsp filling onto each sheet.
  6. There are several ways to seal this, but the easiest I have found is to fold it over like a postage stamp.
  7. You can seal the edges either with water and pinching with your finger or crimp using a fork; I found the fork method to be the most reliable.

4. Freeze

  1. Arrange in a single layer on lined trays.
  2. Freeze 45–60 minutes until firm.
  3. Transfer to freezer containers overnight.
  4. If you want to make these right away skip this step,obviously.😎

5. Cook

  1. Bring well-salted water to a rolling boil.
  2. Cook tortelli directly from frozen.
  3. Once floating (key step), cook 3-4 minutes more, until al dente.
  4. Remove gently with slotted spoon or spider.

6. Brown Butter & Finish

  1. Melt thebutter in wide skillet over medium heat.
  2. Cook until the foaming subsides and it becomes golden brown and nutty.
  3. Add sage and thyme; cook briefly.
  4. Toss the tortelli gently in butter.
  5. Finish with freshly grated Parmigiano and serve immediately.
