Fall Spice Oatmeal Raisin Cookies

Warm, chewy, and heavily spiced cookies—built on a trusted foundation, elevated with Caputo 00 flour, plumped raisins, and autumn's best aromatics.

There is something deeply grounding about starting a bake with ingredients you trust. Taking our cue from a beloved Brown Eyed Baker classic, we set out to build an oatmeal raisin cookie that leans entirely into the cozy, shifting rhythm of fall. While we didn't have molasses or cloves on hand for a true gingerbread mash-up, what emerged from the kitchen was something even better: a pumpkin-pie-meets-oatmeal masterpiece heavily loaded with spice, baked large, and kept wonderfully chewy.

🥣 The Ingredient Lineup

We kept the core measurements of the trusted starting recipe, but upgraded the components:

  • The Flour: Caputo 00 flour (the red bag) for an exceptional, tender crumb.
  • The Oats: Bob's Red Mill old-fashioned oats.
  • The Butter: Rich, premium Kerrygold butter, whipped up effortlessly in the KitchenAid stand mixer.
  • The Raisins: Plumped to perfection by giving them a quick 1-minute water bath in the microwave, patted completely dry before joining the batter.
  • The Spices (The Heavy Hand): About 5 tablespoons of cinnamon, alongside a full tablespoon each of ginger and nutmeg. Adjust to your personal taste, but this robust balance pairs masterfully against the hearty oats and sweet raisins.

👩‍🍳 How We Made Them

  1. Plump the raisins: Toss your raisins into a large measuring cup, cover with water, and microwave for 1 minute to accelerate the plumping process. Pat them dry before folding them in later.
  2. Mix the base: Using your KitchenAid stand mixer, cream the Kerrygold butter and sugars until light and fluffy.
  3. Incorporate wet ingredients: Add your eggs and vanilla, mixing thoroughly.
  4. Blend the dry ingredients: Whisk together the Caputo 00 flour, baking soda, salt, and your heavy-handed ratios of cinnamon, ginger, and nutmeg.
  5. Combine: Gently fold in the Bob's Red Mill oats and your patted-dry, plumped raisins.
  6. Bake large and slow: Scoop slightly larger portions of dough. Because of their size, they take about 28 minutes to bake through while staying delightfully chewy in the center. (Keep an eye on your oven and adjust to your taste!)

🍽️ Enjoy!

Everyone who has tried this batch has fallen in love with the deep autumn spice notes. Whether you enjoy them warm out of the oven with a cup of coffee or packed away for a crisp afternoon walk, they capture the very best of seasonal baking.
