The Grater Good: Choosing Block Cheese

Tips & Tricks · Spring 2026

There is something deeply satisfying about grating cheese fresh from the block. The texture is better, the flavor is richer, and the ingredient list is often much simpler. At Thistle & Cypress, we believe in choosing foods that are minimally processed and made with recognizable ingredients.

Cheese is one of those areas where small changes create a noticeable difference—not just in the nutritional profile, but in how our recipes, like our Refined Egg Cups or seasonal gratins, actually perform in the heat of the oven.

Why We Prefer Block Cheese

Pre-shredded cheeses are an undeniable convenience, but that convenience comes with a literal cost to the texture. To prevent clumping and extend shelf life, commercial shredded cheeses in the United States often contain several additives:

  • Cellulose Powder: A plant fiber used to keep strands from sticking.
  • Potato or Corn Starch: Used as anti-caking agents.
  • Natamycin: A mold inhibitor.
  • Calcium Sulfate & Artificial Colorings: Added for structural and visual consistency.

While these are deemed safe by regulators, they fundamentally change the cheese. These additives can interfere with the moisture balance and melt quality, often resulting in a "grainy" sauce or a topping that browns unevenly. Freshly grated cheese melts into a smooth, cohesive creaminess that pre-packaged shreds simply cannot replicate.

A Note on Cellulose:

Cellulose is frequently derived from wood pulp or plant fibers. While it isn't considered harmful, its presence in food is a hallmark of ultra-processing. In our kitchen, we find that removing this unnecessary filler allows the true character of the dairy to shine.

Buying with Intention

When sourcing cheese for the Thistle & Cypress pantry, we look for a short, recognizable ingredient list: milk, cultures, salt, and enzymes.

Whenever possible, we opt for organic or grass-fed blocks, or support local dairies where the craft of cheesemaking is still held in high regard. We try to avoid "cheese products," added oils, or fillers that mask the natural profile of the cheese.

Kitchen Tips for the Grater

  • The 10-Minute Freeze: If you are working with a softer cheese like Havarti or Mozzarella, place the block in the freezer for 5–10 minutes before grating. This firms up the fats and prevents the cheese from gumming up the grater.
  • The Parchment Trick: Grate directly onto a piece of parchment paper. It makes the transfer to your mixing bowl seamless and keeps your countertops clean.
  • Size Matters: Use the large holes of a box grater for casseroles and egg bites to maintain distinct pockets of flavor, and the fine microplane for a delicate finish on pastas.

Convenience has its place, but for the recipes where texture and flavor truly matter, the five minutes spent at the box grater is an investment in the final dish.
