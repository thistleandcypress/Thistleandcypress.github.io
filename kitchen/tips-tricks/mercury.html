Smart Seafood: Understanding Mercury

Tips & Tricks · Spring 2026

Seafood is an cornerstone of a healthy kitchen, offering an excellent source of lean protein, omega-3 fatty acids, and essential minerals. However, living with intention means being fully aware of the ingredients we bring to our table. One of the most important nuances of seafood consumption is understanding methylmercury.

Mercury is a naturally occurring element, but it can accumulate in fish over time—particularly in larger, longer-lived predatory species. While the benefits of eating fish are significant, consuming it smartly requires a balanced approach based on variety and frequency.

The Yellowfin Distinction

In many of our recipes, such as the Italian Tuna Salad, we often reach for high-quality yellowfin tuna. It is important to note that while yellowfin is delicious and nutrient-dense, it contains higher mercury levels than "light" tunas (such as skipjack).

According to the combined advice of the FDA and the EPA, yellowfin tuna is categorized as a "Good Choice." This means it is safe and healthy to consume, but it should be limited to one serving per week. For those who enjoy tuna more frequently, rotating in lower-mercury options like canned light tuna (skipjack) allows for a better balance.

FDA & EPA Consumption Categories:

  • Best Choices: Eat 2-3 servings a week (e.g., Salmon, Cod, Shrimp, Canned Light Tuna).
  • Good Choices: Eat 1 serving a week (e.g., Yellowfin Tuna, Albacore/White Tuna, Halibut).
  • Choices to Avoid: Highest mercury levels (e.g., King Mackerel, Shark, Swordfish).

Sourcing for Health

The goal isn't to remove these foods from our rhythm, but to purchase and consume them with knowledge. By understanding which species sit higher on the food chain, we can make informed decisions at the fish counter that protect our health without sacrificing the culinary joy of a well-made meal.

Reliable Resources for Food Safety

For those who wish to dive deeper into the specifics of mercury and seafood safety, we recommend the following official resources:

  • FDA/EPA: Advice about Eating Fish - The primary chart for determining serving frequencies.
  • EPA: Choose Fish and Shellfish Wisely - Guidelines on choosing sustainable and safe seafood.
  • CDC: Mercury Exposure Facts - Detailed health information regarding mercury accumulation.
