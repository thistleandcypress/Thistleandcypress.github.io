Agnolotti with Two Fillings & Brown Butter

Handmade agnolotti with two fillings — potato and cheese, and sauerkraut with mushroom — finished in nutty brown butter with sage and thyme.

Agnolotti with Two Fillings & Brown Butter

A New Year's ritual: prepare and freeze handmade agnolotti on New Year's Eve, then cook them gently in brown butter and herbs on New Year's Day. This is pasta as ceremony, comfort, and celebration.

Overview

Handmade agnolotti—a traditional Piedmontese pasta—typically filled with meat. This version takes a different path, using two vegetarian fillings: potato and cheese, and sauerkraut with mushroom. Essentially pierogies in Italian form, and just as comforting. The pasta shape is more basic: folding over like a postage stamp and crimping with a fork.

Finished in nutty brown butter with sage and thyme, these can be made ahead and frozen—a perfect New Year's ritual that turns preparation into celebration.

Ingredients

Dough

  • 400 g Italian 00 flour
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1–2 tsp olive oil (optional – adds richness and helps prevent sticking)
  • Pinch of salt

Potato & Cheese Filling

  • 3–4 lbs potatoes (russet or Yukon Gold)
  • 1 yellow onion, finely diced
  • 2–4 tbsp butter
  • 6–8 oz sharp cheddar or farmer's cheese
  • Salt and black pepper

Sauerkraut & Mushroom Filling

  • 16 oz sauerkraut, drained
  • 8 oz mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 1 yellow onion, finely diced
  • 2–3 tbsp butter (or olive oil for a lighter option)
  • 1–2 tsp caraway seeds (optional)
  • Splash of white wine or broth (optional)
  • Salt and black pepper

Brown Butter Herb Sauce

  • 8–12 tbsp butter
  • 8–12 fresh sage leaves
  • 3–4 sprigs fresh thyme
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Freshly grated Parmigiano Reggiano

Method

1. Make the Dough

  1. Using a baking mat or clean work surface, form the flour into a mound and create a well in the center using two fingers.
  2. Add eggs, salt, and olive oil (if using) into the well.
  3. Beat the eggs carefully with a fork, then slowly start to incorporate the flour from the inner walls of the well, working your way outward.
  4. Don't worry when the dough looks craggy, crumbly, or slightly sticky – this is normal! Now is the time to use your best tool: your hands. Work the remaining flour into the mixture until it comes together.
  5. Adjust the texture as needed: if the dough is too dry or crumbly, add water one tablespoon at a time. If it's too sticky, dust with a small amount of flour.
  6. Knead the dough by hand for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  7. Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and rest the dough for 30–60 minutes (I prefer to rest it on the counter rather than refrigerating).

2. Prepare the Fillings

Potato & Cheese:

  1. Peel and cut potatoes into even chunks. Place in a large pot of cold, salted water.
  2. Bring to a boil and cook 15–20 minutes until fork-tender.
  3. Drain well and let steam dry for a minute.
  4. While potatoes cook, sauté onion in butter over medium heat until translucent, about 5–7 minutes.
  5. Mash potatoes (I prefer using a ricer) and combine with sautéed onion and cheese. The heat from the potatoes will melt the cheese.
  6. Season generously with salt and pepper.
  7. Cool completely before using.

Sauerkraut & Mushroom:

  1. Heat butter (or olive oil) in a large skillet over medium-high heat.
  2. Add onion and cook until softened, about 5 minutes.
  3. Add mushrooms and cook until browned and liquid has evaporated, about 8–10 minutes.
  4. Add sauerkraut and optional seasonings (caraway seeds, splash of white wine or broth).
  5. Cook 5–10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  6. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
  7. Cool completely before using.

3. Roll & Shape

  1. Divide dough into 4 pieces. Keep unused portions covered.
  2. Dust your baking mat or work surface with semolina so the pasta doesn't stick.
  3. Roll dough thin using a pasta machine, starting with setting 1 and gradually increasing to setting 6–7.
  4. Cut into 2–2.5 inch squares (I prefer to use a ravioli stamp).
  5. Spoon 1 tsp filling onto the center of each square.
  6. Fold over like a postage stamp to form a triangle.
  7. Seal the edges with a bit of water and crimp using a fork – I've found this to be the most reliable method.

4. Freeze

  1. Arrange agnolotti in a single layer on parchment-lined trays.
  2. Freeze 45–60 minutes until firm.
  3. Transfer to freezer containers or bags for longer storage.
  4. If you want to make these right away, skip this step. 😎

5. Cook

  1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil.
  2. Cook agnolotti directly from frozen (do not thaw).
  3. Once they float to the surface (key step!), cook 3–4 minutes more until al dente.
  4. Remove gently with a slotted spoon or spider.

6. Brown Butter & Finish

  1. Melt butter in a wide skillet over medium heat.
  2. Cook until the foaming subsides and the butter becomes golden brown and nutty-smelling.
  3. Add sage and thyme; cook until fragrant and crispy, about 30 seconds.
  4. Add the cooked agnolotti and toss gently to coat.
  5. Finish with freshly grated Parmigiano Reggiano and serve immediately.

7. Watch everyone go quiet when they take that first bite

New Year's Day never tasted so good.
